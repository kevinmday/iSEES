\# Knowledge Governance



\## Purpose



This document establishes the canonical governance model for knowledge

within the iSEES project.



It defines how knowledge is proposed, reviewed, ratified, evolved,

deprecated, and preserved.



Knowledge governance ensures that architectural evolution remains

deliberate, deterministic, and traceable rather than emerging through

ad hoc documentation or implementation.



\---



\# Architectural Principle



Knowledge shall evolve through disciplined architectural refinement.



Implementation shall follow architecture.



Documentation shall reflect architecture.



Architecture shall never be inferred solely from implementation.



\---



\# Knowledge Lifecycle



Every significant architectural idea progresses through a defined

lifecycle.



```text

Observation



↓



Proposal



↓



Architectural Discussion



↓



Engineering Validation



↓



Canonical Specification



↓



Implementation



↓



Maintenance



↓



Historical Reference

```



Each stage serves a distinct purpose.



Knowledge advances only when sufficient understanding has been achieved.



\---



\# Lifecycle Stages



\## Observation



A new insight, problem, or opportunity is identified.



Questions are encouraged.



No architectural commitment exists.



\---



\## Proposal



A potential architectural direction is documented.



Alternatives may exist.



Tradeoffs are explored.



The proposal is intentionally provisional.



\---



\## Architectural Discussion



The proposal is evaluated against existing Canon.



Responsibilities are clarified.



Boundaries are refined.



Architectural consistency is prioritized over implementation

convenience.



\---



\## Engineering Validation



Prototype implementations, experiments, and engineering work determine

whether the architecture behaves as intended.



Engineering informs architecture.



Engineering does not replace architecture.



\---



\## Canonical Specification



Once validated, the knowledge becomes part of the System Canon or other

appropriate canonical documentation.



Canonical knowledge becomes the governing reference.



\---



\## Implementation



Software realizes the Canon.



Implementation details may change without changing the governing

architecture.



\---



\## Maintenance



Canonical knowledge evolves through controlled revision.



Corrections improve clarity without altering architectural intent.



Substantive changes require deliberate architectural review.



\---



\## Historical Reference



Superseded documents are preserved when they explain architectural

evolution.



Historical documents provide lineage rather than authority.



\---



\# Responsibilities



\## System Canon



Defines permanent computational and cognitive principles.



\---



\## Engineering Architecture



Implements Canon.



\---



\## Research Methodology



Defines disciplined investigative practice.



\---



\## Domain Knowledge



Provides the subject matter investigated by iSEES.



\---



\## Historical Doctrine



Preserves architectural evolution.



\---



\# Change Control



Every architectural change shall answer four questions:



1\. What problem does this solve?



2\. Why is existing architecture insufficient?



3\. Which knowledge domain owns this change?



4\. Does this strengthen or weaken architectural clarity?



Changes that increase ambiguity should be rejected or revised.



\---



\# Canon Evolution



Canonical documents are expected to evolve.



Evolution should reduce complexity rather than increase it.



Architectural refinement is preferred over architectural expansion.



The number of documents is less important than the clarity of their

responsibilities.



\---



\# Governance Principles



Knowledge ownership shall remain singular.



Architectural responsibilities shall remain explicit.



Historical context shall never be discarded.



Implementation shall remain subordinate to architecture.



Knowledge shall remain inspectable.



Knowledge shall remain reproducible.



Knowledge shall remain deterministic.



\---



\# Architectural Invariants



Knowledge governance exists to preserve long-term architectural

integrity.



No engineering deadline, implementation shortcut, or technology choice

shall redefine canonical knowledge without deliberate architectural

review.



Architecture leads.



Engineering follows.



\---



\# Conclusion



Knowledge Governance provides the disciplined process through which the

iSEES architecture evolves.



Rather than allowing documentation and implementation to drift together,

the project maintains a clear separation between architectural intent,

engineering realization, and historical evolution.



This governance model ensures that future growth strengthens the

architecture while preserving the clarity, determinism, and coherence

upon which iSEES is founded.

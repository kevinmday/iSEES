\# Initial Architectural Assessment



\## Resolve–Dissolve Computation



\### Description



Resolve–Dissolve Computation models investigation as a deterministic computational process.



Rather than treating investigative graphs as static knowledge structures, RDC constructs an Investigation Manifold through deterministic computation over an explicitly defined computational universe.



\---



\### Current Governing Equation



The canonical reduction of RDC is expressed as:



\*\*M = g(L,T,S)\*\*



Where:



\*\*M\*\*



Investigation Manifold



\*\*g\*\*



Resolve–Dissolve Computational Operator



\*\*L\*\*



Active Computational Layers



\*\*T\*\*



Temporal Context



\*\*S\*\*



Investigative Scale



This equation represents the governing equation of Resolve–Dissolve Computation.



It serves as the canonical reduction of the RDC architecture.



\---



\### Initial Assessment



Current understanding suggests:



\- Mathematical notation itself is well established.

\- Deterministic computation is well established.

\- Knowledge graphs are well established.

\- Provenance systems are well established.

\- Runtime architectures are well established.



The current hypothesis is that the potential novelty does \*\*not\*\* reside within these individual concepts.



Instead, it may reside within their architectural synthesis.

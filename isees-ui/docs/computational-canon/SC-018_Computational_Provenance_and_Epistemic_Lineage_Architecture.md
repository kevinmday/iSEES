\# 1. Abstract



This canon establishes Computational Provenance and Epistemic Lineage as the

canonical mechanisms through which iSEES preserves the historical evolution of

computational understanding.



Unlike traditional document systems, provenance within iSEES is not limited to

authorship or modification history.



Instead, provenance SHALL preserve the computational evolution of ideas,

discoveries, interpretations, and promoted knowledge.



The objective is to preserve not only what is known, but how that knowledge

came to exist.



\---



\# 2. Motivation



Scientific investigation is cumulative.



Every new understanding is built upon previous observations, previous

investigations, previous hypotheses, and previous computational knowledge.



Traditional systems preserve:



\- file history,

\- document authorship,

\- revision timestamps.



These are insufficient.



Researchers must also understand:



\- where an idea originated,

\- which observations enabled it,

\- who expanded it,

\- who challenged it,

\- who validated it,

\- who promoted it into canonical knowledge.



The history of understanding itself therefore becomes a computational artifact.



\---



\# 3. Fundamental Principle



The purpose of provenance is not attribution alone.



The purpose of provenance is reconstruction of the computational evolution of

understanding.



\---



\# 4. Provenance



Every Computational Artifact SHALL possess provenance.



Examples include:



\- Observations

\- Evidence

\- Relationships

\- Narratives

\- Knowledge Objects

\- Author Documents

\- Presentations

\- Future computational artifacts



Provenance SHALL remain computationally inspectable.



\---



\# 5. Epistemic Lineage



Epistemic Lineage represents the chain of computational understanding from

which a computational artifact emerged.



Illustrative lineage:



```

Observation



↓



Relationship



↓



Narrative



↓



Knowledge Object



↓



Publication

```



Each transition remains computationally preserved.



\---



\# 6. Origin



Every computational artifact SHALL possess an Origin.



Origin identifies:



\- originating manifold,

\- originating investigation,

\- originating researcher,

\- originating organization,

\- originating observation,

\- originating Epistemic State.



Origin SHALL remain immutable.



\---



\# 7. Expansion



Scientific understanding evolves through expansion.



Expansion represents new computational understanding built upon previous work.



Expansion does not replace Origin.



Expansion extends Origin.



Illustrative example:



```

Kevin



↓



Radar Characteristics



↓



Bob



↓



Radar Timing Correlation



↓



Organization



↓



Canonical Knowledge

```



The computational lineage preserves every contribution.



\---



\# 8. Derivation



Knowledge frequently emerges through computational derivation.



Derived artifacts SHALL preserve references to every significant computational

ancestor contributing to the resulting understanding.



Derivation SHALL remain computationally inspectable.



\---



\# 9. Promotion



Promotion represents an epistemic transition rather than merely a workflow

transition.



Example:



```

Research Candidate



↓



Candidate Review



↓



Knowledge Object

```



The resulting Knowledge Object SHALL preserve complete lineage back to the

originating observations.



Promotion SHALL NEVER erase investigative history.



\---



\# 10. Publication



Publication creates a new computational artifact.



Publication SHALL preserve:



\- referenced Knowledge Objects,

\- Epistemic Bindings,

\- originating manifolds,

\- supporting investigations,

\- contributor lineage.



Publication SHALL NOT flatten provenance into simple citations.



\---



\# 11. Federation



Epistemic Lineage naturally extends across federated manifolds.



Example:



```

Kevin Manifold



↓



Knowledge Object



↓



Referenced



↓



Organization Manifold



↓



Referenced



↓



Author Document

```



Ownership remains independent.



Lineage remains continuous.



Federation therefore preserves understanding rather than duplicating

information.



\---



\# 12. Attribution



Attribution is one component of provenance.



It is not the entirety of provenance.



Researchers may contribute through:



\- original observation,

\- expansion,

\- relationship discovery,

\- narrative development,

\- hypothesis formulation,

\- validation,

\- review,

\- promotion.



Each contribution SHALL remain computationally visible.



\---



\# 13. Historical Reconstruction



Researchers SHALL be capable of reconstructing the complete epistemic history

of any computational artifact.



Questions such as:



\- Who first observed this?

\- Which investigation discovered this relationship?

\- Which researcher expanded the original understanding?

\- Which organization promoted it?

\- Which publication first referenced it?



SHALL be computationally answerable.



\---



\# 14. REX Integration



REX SHALL operate upon computational provenance rather than document history.



Examples include:



\- Origin Graph

\- Expansion Graph

\- Derivation Graph

\- Promotion Graph

\- Publication Graph



REX therefore reconstructs the evolution of understanding rather than merely

the evolution of files.



\---



\# 15. Projection Preservation



Projection SHALL preserve provenance.



Examples include:



\- .author

\- PDF

\- DOCX

\- HTML

\- Markdown

\- Future computational projections



Every projection SHALL preserve sufficient provenance to reconstruct the

computational origins of the published work.



Where projection formats possess limited expressive capability, provenance

SHALL be represented using appendices, metadata, embedded computational

references, or equivalent mechanisms.



Projection SHALL NEVER intentionally destroy provenance.



\---



\# 16. Computational Principles



The following principles are normative.



1\. Every Computational Artifact SHALL possess provenance.



2\. Provenance SHALL preserve computational understanding rather than merely

authorship.



3\. Origin SHALL remain immutable.



4\. Expansion SHALL preserve previous understanding.



5\. Promotion SHALL preserve investigative history.



6\. Publication SHALL preserve computational lineage.



7\. Federation SHALL preserve ownership without disrupting provenance.



8\. Projection SHALL preserve provenance whenever computationally possible.



9\. Researchers SHALL be capable of reconstructing historical understanding.



10\. Epistemic Lineage SHALL remain computationally inspectable.



\---



\# 17. Architectural Summary



Computational Provenance and Epistemic Lineage establish the historical memory

of the iSEES platform.



Rather than preserving only documents or revisions, iSEES preserves the

computational evolution of understanding itself.



Observations become discoveries.



Discoveries become knowledge.



Knowledge becomes publications.



Publications preserve the complete lineage of computational reasoning from

which they emerged.



Together these principles establish the canonical provenance architecture upon

which trustworthy computational research is built.

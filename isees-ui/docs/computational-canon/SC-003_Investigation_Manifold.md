\# INVESTIGATION MANIFOLD

\# ============================================================



\## Abstract



The Investigation Manifold is the primary computational product of

Resolve–Dissolve Computation (RDC).



It is not a graph.



It is not a visualization.



It is not merely a collection of nodes and edges.



The Investigation Manifold is a deterministic topological model that

emerges from the interaction of computational layers, investigative

scale, temporal context, and the available body of evidence.



Every analytical surface within iSEES is ultimately a projection of the

Investigation Manifold.



\---



\# 1. Motivation



Traditional investigations organize evidence into reports, databases,

timelines, maps, or network diagrams.



Each representation reveals only one perspective.



The Investigation Manifold unifies all perspectives into a single

deterministic computational object.



Rather than asking:



"What report should I open?"



the operator asks:



"What does the manifold reveal?"



\---



\# 2. Definition



The Investigation Manifold is defined as



\\\[

M = g(L,T,S)

\\]



This is the general event-space manifold construction law. A manifold
instantiated under governed evidence context E is written

\\[

M_E = g(L_E,T_E,S_E)

\\]

where



\\\[

L

=

\\text{Active Computational Layers}

\\]



\\\[

T

=

\\text{Temporal Context}

\\]



\\\[

S

=

\\text{Investigative Scale}

\\]



\\\[

E

=

\\text{Governed Evidence Context}

\\]



The subscripted terms are the layer configuration L_E, temporal context
T_E, and investigative scale or scope S_E selected, populated, or
resolved under governed evidence context E. Evidence therefore affects
the manifold being instantiated through governed investigation state. E
is an indexing or conditioning context, not a fourth peer argument
supplied directly to g.

The construction operator g remains invariant. Different evidence
contexts may yield different instantiated manifolds (E1 != E2 may
produce M_E1 != M_E2) without introducing a new construction operator.

The former notation M=g(L,T,S,E) is superseded by the two-level notation
above because the four-argument form was ambiguous. This clarification
preserves its historical proposition that evidence affects the
instantiated manifold. Neither expression is a similarity or
correspondence equation, weighted aggregate, Resolve candidate score,
INTENTION governing model, or statement of probability, confidence,
causation, proof, or truth.



\---



\# 3. Properties



The Investigation Manifold possesses several defining properties.



It is



• deterministic



• reproducible



• inspectable



• topology-driven



• evidence-based



• continuously recomputable



Every manifold can be reconstructed from identical inputs.



\---



\# 4. Emergence



The manifold is not manually constructed.



It emerges naturally through Resolve–Dissolve Computation.



During recomputation



• nodes may emerge



• nodes may disappear



• edges may strengthen



• edges may weaken



• clusters may merge



• clusters may divide



• new hypotheses may naturally appear



The topology is therefore considered emergent rather than prescribed.

\# 11. Relationship to RDC



Resolve–Dissolve Computation owns the mathematical process that produces

the Investigation Manifold.



The Investigation Manifold is the deterministic output of RDC.



The manifold itself performs no computation.



It represents the computational state of the current investigation.



\---



\# 12. Relationship to the Intention Engine



The Investigation Manifold supplies deterministic topology and topology

metrics to the Intention Engine.



The Intention Engine does not modify the manifold.



Instead it consumes manifold-derived structure to evaluate candidate

intentions.



The relationship is therefore



Resolve–Dissolve Computation



↓



Investigation Manifold



↓



Topology Metrics



↓



Intention Engine

\---



\# Conclusion



The Investigation Manifold represents a fundamental departure from

traditional investigative software.



Rather than organizing evidence into disconnected reports, timelines,

maps, and graphs, iSEES constructs a single deterministic computational

topology from which every analytical view is derived.



As computational assumptions evolve through Resolve–Dissolve

Computation, the manifold evolves with them, allowing new structures,

relationships, contradictions, and hypotheses to emerge naturally.



The Investigation Manifold is therefore not simply the center of the

user interface—it is the computational heart of every investigation

performed within iSEES.

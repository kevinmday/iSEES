\# SC-013 — Computational Observation Architecture



\## Foundational Canon



\### Abstract



Computational systems do not directly observe reality. They receive observations emitted by entities capable of observing some portion of reality.



This canon establishes the \*\*Computational Observation Architecture\*\* as the exclusive computational gateway through which all external information enters iSEES.



The architecture separates observations from evidence, evidence from knowledge, and knowledge from publication, thereby preserving provenance, uncertainty, reproducibility, and deterministic computation.



This architecture is domain independent and applies equally to scientific research, intelligence analysis, medicine, engineering, historical preservation, environmental monitoring, and every future computational investigation.



\---



\# 1. Architectural Principle



Reality SHALL NOT enter iSEES directly.



Reality SHALL be represented exclusively through \*\*Computational Observations\*\*.



No subsystem shall bypass this architecture.



\---



\# 2. Observation



An Observation is the smallest atomic computational unit capable of entering the computational runtime.



An Observation represents that:



> Some Observation Source observed something.



An Observation DOES NOT imply:



\* truth

\* correctness

\* evidence

\* confidence

\* causality



It represents only that an observation occurred.



\---



\# 3. Observation Sources



An Observation Source is any entity capable of emitting observations.



Examples include:



\* Human operators

\* Scientific instruments

\* Aircraft telemetry

\* Marine AIS

\* Weather stations

\* Satellites

\* Government publications

\* Social platforms

\* Research papers

\* Sensors

\* Cameras

\* AI systems

\* Future observation technologies



Observation Sources SHALL NOT be computationally privileged.



All Observation Sources participate identically.



\---



\# 4. Canonical Observation



Regardless of origin, every Observation SHALL normalize into a canonical computational representation.



The canonical representation SHALL be independent of:



\* file format

\* transport protocol

\* platform

\* programming language

\* vendor

\* acquisition method



\---



\# 5. Computational Observation Runtime



The Computational Observation Runtime SHALL own:



\* acquisition

\* authentication

\* normalization

\* provenance

\* temporal ordering

\* routing

\* deduplication

\* licensing

\* observation identity

\* observation revision



The runtime SHALL NOT:



\* compute hypotheses

\* infer conclusions

\* alter investigations

\* modify manifold topology



Those responsibilities belong to downstream computational runtimes.



\---



\# 6. Observation Bus



All observations SHALL enter the platform through a single computational abstraction:



\*\*Observation Bus\*\*



Observation Sources SHALL publish.



Computational consumers SHALL subscribe.



Observation Sources SHALL NEVER communicate directly with investigations.



\---



\# 7. Investigation Subscription



Investigations SHALL subscribe to observations.



Investigations SHALL NOT poll observation sources directly.



Subscriptions may include:



\* spatial constraints

\* temporal constraints

\* semantic constraints

\* source constraints

\* confidence constraints

\* computational intentions



Investigations therefore become continuously observing computational systems rather than static repositories.



\---

The following computational hierarchy SHALL remain invariant.



```text

Observation



↓

↓

↓



Hypotheses

Intentions

Knowledge
```

No computational stage may be skipped.

\# 9. Observation Provenance



Every Observation SHALL preserve:



\* original source

\* acquisition timestamp

\* observation timestamp (when known)

\* acquisition method

\* transformation history

\* revision history

\* licensing

\* computational lineage



No Observation shall lose provenance.

\---



\# 14. Fundamental Computational Law



\### Law 13.1



> \*\*All external information SHALL enter iSEES exclusively as Computational Observations.\*\*



No external entity may create evidence, hypotheses, relationships, manifold topology, or investigative conclusions directly.



Those structures emerge solely through deterministic computation performed within the computational runtime.



\---



\# 15. Computational Observation Philosophy



Unlike traditional information systems, iSEES does not ingest facts.



Facts are not computational primitives.



Evidence is not a computational primitive.



Knowledge is not a computational primitive.



Instead, iSEES continuously acquires observations from the world and deterministically transforms those observations into higher-order computational structures through provenance-preserving computation.



Accordingly:



\* Observations become Evidence.

\* Evidence forms Relationships.

\* Relationships support Hypotheses.

\* Hypotheses resolve Intentions.

\* Intentions expand Computational Manifolds.

\* Computational Manifolds project Knowledge back into the world.



This separation preserves uncertainty, enables reproducibility, and allows the computational model to evolve continuously as new observations arrive.



The world is therefore not periodically imported into iSEES.



The world is continuously observed.



iSEES does not store reality.



\*\*It continuously computes an evolving, provenance-aware representation of reality from the observations it receives.\*\*

\# SC-014 — Computational Authoring and Narrative Architecture



\*\*Status:\*\* Canonical

\*\*Classification:\*\* Foundational Computational Architecture

\*\*Depends On:\*\* SC-003 Investigation Manifold, SC-010 Investigative Provenance and Graph Revision Architecture, SC-011 Investigative Expansion and Event-Space Reconstruction, SC-012 Computational Projection Architecture, SC-013 Computational Observation Architecture



\---



\# 1. Purpose



This canon establishes the computational architecture governing how investigations become authored knowledge.



Traditional authoring systems regard text as the primary artifact and knowledge as something inferred from written language. This architecture reverses that assumption.



Within iSEES, authored knowledge is not fundamentally composed of words.



It is composed of computational observations, relationships, artifacts, hypotheses, and authorial intent.



Narrative language is a projection generated from those computational structures.



The author constructs knowledge.



Language communicates that knowledge.



\---



\# 2. Architectural Principle



The computational document is the canonical authored artifact.



Human-readable prose is only one possible realization of that artifact.



The document therefore exists independently of any individual editor, language model, export format, or visual representation.



Multiple narrative projections may be generated from an identical computational document without altering the underlying investigative knowledge.



\---



\# 3. Observation Before Language



Every investigation begins with observation.



Observations may originate from:



\* investigative research

\* computational expansion

\* imported artifacts

\* interviews

\* sensor systems

\* imagery

\* documents

\* external repositories

\* future observation providers



These observations become first-class computational objects inside the investigation manifold.



They are never reduced to plain text.



\---



\# 4. Research Collection



During investigation the researcher traverses the manifold rather than writes prose.



Nodes, edges, timelines, artifacts, hypotheses, and relationships are intentionally collected into the Research Workspace.



This collection represents investigative intent rather than authored language.



The collected objects remain computationally connected to the investigation from which they originated.



No duplication occurs.



Research collection is therefore an act of computational organization rather than textual note taking.



\---



\# 5. Computational Authoring



When Studio is entered, the researcher does not begin by writing paragraphs.



Instead, the researcher constructs a computational document.



This construction consists of arranging observations, artifacts, relationships, timelines, and other computational objects into an intentional narrative structure.



The author's primary activity becomes organization rather than composition.



Knowledge is assembled before language exists.



\---



\# 6. Computational Narrative Objects



The smallest authored unit is not a paragraph.



It is a Computational Narrative.



A Computational Narrative references one or more computational observations and preserves the relationships that produced its narrative.



A Computational Narrative may contain references to:



\* observation nodes

\* graph edges

\* artifacts

\* timelines

\* hypotheses

\* citations

\* investigations

\* computational prompts

\* projection metadata

\* provenance references



The visible paragraph is only one projection of this computational structure.



\---



\# 7. AI as a Projection Engine



Artificial Intelligence is not the author.



Artificial Intelligence is one possible projection engine.



The researcher remains responsible for selecting observations, arranging evidence, defining intent, and determining investigative direction.



AI assists by transforming computational structures into natural language when requested.



For example, the researcher may arrange several observations concerning radar evidence and request a technical narrative describing those observations.



The generated prose remains computationally linked to every originating observation.



Removing or replacing generated language never destroys the underlying knowledge structure.



\---



\# 8. Observation-Driven Narrative Generation



Narrative generation operates upon computational objects rather than free-form prompts alone.



A researcher may select:



\* one observation

\* multiple observations

\* graph relationships

\* timelines

\* artifacts

\* mixed computational collections



The projection engine interprets their semantic relationships and generates narrative appropriate to the requested projection style.



Possible projections include, but are not limited to:



\* investigative

\* scientific

\* legal

\* academic

\* executive

\* congressional

\* journalistic

\* technical

\* public

\* educational



The computational document remains unchanged.



Only the projected narrative differs.



\---



\# 9. Persistent Computational Identity



Every generated narrative retains persistent computational identity.



Each narrative projection maintains references to the computational objects from which it originated.



Consequently every sentence may answer:



\* Which observations support this statement?

\* Which artifacts contributed?

\* Which graph relationships were involved?

\* Which investigation revision produced this paragraph?

\* Which prompt generated this projection?



Narrative therefore possesses deterministic provenance.



\---



\# 10. Living Documents



A computational document remains alive throughout the investigation lifecycle.



When additional observations become available, the system may determine that existing narrative projections no longer represent the complete investigative state.



Rather than silently modifying authored text, Studio informs the researcher that new supporting or contradictory observations exist.



The researcher decides whether projections should be regenerated.



Knowledge evolves.



Authorship remains under human control.



\---



\# 11. Separation of Knowledge and Language



This architecture deliberately separates knowledge from language.



Knowledge exists within the investigation manifold.



Language exists within narrative projections.



Because these layers remain independent:



\* multiple narratives may originate from identical observations

\* projections may be regenerated at any time

\* language styles may change without altering knowledge

\* translation becomes projection rather than rewriting

\* computational provenance is preserved regardless of language



Knowledge therefore becomes editor-independent and language-independent.



\---



\# 12. Computational Review



Because narrative remains connected to computational structure, Studio may analyze authored documents using deterministic computational methods rather than language generation alone.



Examples include:



\* unsupported assertions

\* missing observations

\* contradictory evidence

\* weak investigative coverage

\* provenance completeness

\* graph connectivity

\* narrative consistency

\* timeline completeness

\* evidence density

\* unresolved hypotheses



These analyses evaluate the computational document rather than merely the visible prose.



\---



\# 13. Relationship to Projection Architecture



SC-012 establishes that every representation of a computational document is a projection.



This canon extends that principle.



Narrative itself is a projection.



Human-readable language therefore occupies the same architectural role as:



\* PDF

\* DOCX

\* HTML

\* Markdown

\* Presentation

\* Visualization



Natural language becomes another computational realization of the canonical authored document.



\---



\# 14. Relationship to Observation Architecture



SC-013 defines computational observations.



This canon defines how those observations become authored investigative knowledge.



Observation is therefore the input.



Narrative is the output.



The computational document is the canonical transformation between them.



\---



\# 15. Canonical Workflow



The canonical investigative workflow becomes:



World Observation



↓



Computational Observation



↓



Investigation Manifold



↓



Research Collection



↓



Authorial Arrangement



↓



Computational Document



↓



Narrative Projection



↓



Human Reader



The author constructs knowledge through computational organization.



Narrative emerges as one projection of that knowledge.



\---



\# 16. Foundational Principle



The purpose of Studio is not to help humans write.



Its purpose is to help humans construct computational knowledge.



Writing becomes one expression of that knowledge rather than its definition.



This distinction establishes the Computational Authoring and Narrative Architecture as the canonical bridge between investigative observation and human understanding within the iSEES platform.

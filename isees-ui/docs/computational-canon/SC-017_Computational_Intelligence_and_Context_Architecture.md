\# SC-017 — Computational Intelligence and Context Architecture



\*\*Status:\*\* Canonical

\*\*Classification:\*\* Foundational Computational Architecture

\*\*Depends On:\*\* SC-003 Investigation Manifold, SC-009 Manifold Instrument Architecture, SC-013 Computational Observation Architecture, SC-014 Computational Authoring and Narrative Architecture, SC-016 Human-Computational Collaborative Authoring Architecture



\---



\# 1. Purpose



This canon establishes the computational architecture governing how computational intelligence acquires context, performs reasoning, and assists researchers within the iSEES platform.



Within iSEES, intelligence does not reason from prompts alone.



Instead, every reasoning activity begins with the deterministic assembly of computational context from the current investigative state.



Reasoning therefore becomes context-aware rather than prompt-driven.



\---



\# 2. Fundamental Principle



Computational intelligence shall reason from computational context.



Natural-language prompts express user intent.



Computational context supplies investigative knowledge.



Neither is sufficient alone.



The quality of reasoning depends upon both.



\---



\# 3. Computational Context



Computational context is the structured collection of computational knowledge relevant to a reasoning task.



Context may include:



\* current document

\* current selection

\* computational observations

\* graph nodes

\* graph edges

\* artifacts

\* hypotheses

\* timelines

\* provenance

\* author intent

\* publication intent

\* investigative history



Computational context is assembled before any reasoning instrument is invoked.



\---



\# 4. Context Assembly



Context is assembled incrementally.



Reasoning begins with the smallest relevant computational neighborhood and expands only when additional knowledge is required.



A typical expansion sequence is:



Current Selection



↓



Current Paragraph



↓



Current Computational Document



↓



Research Collection



↓



Current Investigation



↓



Current Manifold Neighborhood



↓



Account-Owned Manifolds



↓



Shared Manifolds



↓



Public Computational Sources



↓



General World Knowledge



Each expansion increases available knowledge while preserving investigative relevance.



\---



\# 5. Account-Owned Knowledge



Every account possesses one or more computational manifolds representing accumulated investigative knowledge.



These manifolds become first-class reasoning resources.



Computational intelligence may therefore answer questions concerning:



\* previous investigations

\* collected observations

\* prior hypotheses

\* historical conclusions

\* unresolved questions

\* recurring investigative patterns

\* previously imported artifacts



Reasoning is grounded in the researcher's own computational knowledge before consulting external information.



\---



\# 6. Context Objects



Reasoning operates upon Context Objects rather than free-form prompts.



A Context Object may contain:



\* computational observations

\* graph relationships

\* artifacts

\* timelines

\* hypotheses

\* computational narratives

\* publication targets

\* reasoning objectives

\* author preferences

\* provenance references



Every computational instrument receives a structured Context Object appropriate to its responsibility.



\---



\# 7. Computational Intelligence



Computational intelligence is a platform capability rather than a single technology.



Intelligence providers may include:



\* language models

\* symbolic reasoning systems

\* deterministic algorithms

\* graph analytics

\* optimization engines

\* machine learning systems

\* future reasoning architectures



The computational architecture remains independent of implementation.



\---



\# 8. Computational Instruments



Computational intelligence is exposed through specialized computational instruments.



Each instrument performs a narrowly defined responsibility.



Examples include:



\* narrative projection

\* hypothesis generation

\* investigative expansion

\* citation assistance

\* contradiction analysis

\* evidence verification

\* computational review

\* publication preparation



Instruments reason using Context Objects rather than isolated prompts.



\---



\# 9. Context-Aware Dialogue



Conversational interaction is one projection of computational reasoning.



Questions posed by the researcher are interpreted as requests for computational assistance rather than unrestricted conversation.



Responses are grounded in assembled computational context.



Consequently, dialogue becomes investigation-aware, document-aware, and author-aware.



\---



\# 10. Reasoning Boundaries



Computational intelligence may:



\* explain observations

\* summarize investigations

\* generate narratives

\* identify contradictions

\* discover investigative gaps

\* propose hypotheses

\* recommend supporting evidence

\* reorganize computational structures



Computational intelligence shall not:



\* silently modify canonical knowledge

\* fabricate computational observations

\* alter provenance

\* replace human judgment

\* publish without authorization



Reasoning therefore remains advisory.



\---



\# 11. Context Expansion



Reasoning context expands intentionally.



Expansion occurs only when the currently assembled computational neighborhood cannot adequately satisfy the requested reasoning objective.



Expansion therefore remains computationally efficient while preserving investigative focus.



This principle aligns computational intelligence with neighborhood expansion rather than unrestricted corpus traversal.



\---



\# 12. Context Provenance



Every reasoning activity maintains computational provenance.



Researchers may determine:



\* participating computational observations

\* participating investigations

\* participating manifolds

\* reasoning instruments

\* reasoning revision

\* generated computational proposals



Reasoning therefore remains inspectable.



\---



\# 13. Relationship to Investigation



The Investigation Manifold represents accumulated investigative knowledge.



This canon defines how computational intelligence acquires and reasons over that knowledge.



The manifold stores knowledge.



Computational intelligence interprets knowledge.



\---



\# 14. Relationship to Authoring



SC-014 establishes computational authoring.



This canon enables context-aware computational assistance during authoring.



Authoring therefore becomes an intelligent collaboration grounded in computational knowledge rather than isolated prompts.



\---



\# 15. Relationship to Collaboration



SC-016 defines collaboration between researchers and computational instruments.



This canon defines how those instruments acquire the computational knowledge necessary to participate meaningfully in that collaboration.

The computational reasoning lifecycle becomes:



Researcher Intent



↓



Context Assembly



↓



Context Expansion



↓



Context Object



↓



Computational Instrument



↓



Computational Proposal



↓



Human Evaluation



↓



Canonical Document



Reasoning therefore becomes a computational service grounded in structured investigative knowledge.



\---



\# 17. Foundational Principle



The purpose of computational intelligence within iSEES is not to replace human investigation.



Its purpose is to amplify human reasoning by operating upon computational knowledge that has already been intentionally constructed, organized, and preserved.



The researcher supplies intent.



The platform supplies context.



Computational intelligence supplies reasoning.



Together they produce knowledge that remains transparent, inspectable, reproducible, and computationally grounded.

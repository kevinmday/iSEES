\# 1. Abstract



This canon establishes Computational Epistemic Binding as the canonical

mechanism through which computational artifacts reference one another within

iSEES.



Unlike traditional document systems, computational references SHALL NOT be

treated as copied information.



Instead, references SHALL represent intentional epistemic relationships between

evolving bodies of computational knowledge.



An Epistemic Binding preserves the relationship between reasoning and the

knowledge upon which that reasoning depends.



\---



\# 2. Motivation



Traditional documents copy information.



Traditional citations identify sources.



Traditional hyperlinks identify locations.



None preserve the evolving computational relationship between knowledge,

reasoning, and discovery.



Scientific investigation requires more.



Researchers must understand not only what was referenced, but:



\- what was known,

\- when it was known,

\- whose work informed the conclusion,

\- whether understanding has since evolved,

\- and whether subsequent reasoning should be reconsidered.



Epistemic Binding provides this capability.



\---



\# 3. Computational References



A Computational Reference is not a hyperlink.



It is not a copied artifact.



It is not embedded content.



A Computational Reference represents an intentional relationship between two

computational artifacts.



Examples include:



\- Author Documents

\- Knowledge Objects

\- Narratives

\- Investigations

\- Presentations

\- Future Computational Objects



Every Computational Reference SHALL possess an Epistemic Binding.



\---



\# 4. Fundamental Principle



Computational references SHALL bind to evolving understanding rather than

copied information.



The purpose of referencing is not duplication.



The purpose of referencing is preserving computational reasoning.



\---



\# 5. Epistemic State



Every Computational Knowledge Object evolves through time.



Each revision represents a new Epistemic State.



An Epistemic State represents the complete computational understanding of an

artifact at a particular point in investigative history.



Epistemic States preserve:



\- content

\- relationships

\- provenance

\- confidence

\- supporting evidence

\- computational lineage



Reasoning always occurs relative to an Epistemic State.



\---



\# 6. LIVE Binding



A LIVE Binding intentionally follows the current Epistemic State of the

referenced computational artifact.



Example:



```

Radar Characteristics



Binding



LIVE

```



Subsequent revisions immediately become available to dependent computational

artifacts.



LIVE Binding represents continuing trust in the ongoing evolution of knowledge.



LIVE Binding is expected during active investigation and authoring.



\---



\# 7. LOCKED Binding



A LOCKED Binding intentionally preserves the Epistemic State upon which

reasoning was performed.



Example:



```

Radar Characteristics



Binding



LOCKED



Epistemic State



2029-01-12

```



Subsequent revisions SHALL NOT alter the computational reasoning represented by

the dependent artifact.



LOCKED Binding preserves reproducibility.



\---



\# 8. Meaning of LOCKED



LOCKED does not preserve a copy.



LOCKED preserves an epistemic commitment.



It answers the question:



> Upon what understanding was this conclusion intentionally based?



The objective is not software versioning.



The objective is historical reconstruction of reasoning.



\---



\# 9. Research Workflow



During active investigation researchers typically operate using LIVE

Bindings.



As computational understanding evolves, dependent investigations immediately

benefit from improved knowledge.



Researchers intentionally decide when computational reasoning should become

stable.



\---



\# 10. Publication Workflow



Before publication computational references SHOULD transition into LOCKED

Bindings.



Examples include:



\- reports

\- papers

\- presentations

\- exported documents

\- evidence packages

\- legal submissions



Published reasoning must remain reproducible.



Subsequent improvements to referenced knowledge SHALL remain discoverable

without silently altering published conclusions.



\---



\# 11. Re-evaluation



A LOCKED Binding may indicate that newer Epistemic States exist.



The computational system may inform the operator:



```

New Epistemic State Available



Current



2029-01-12



Latest



2029-07-03

```



The operator intentionally determines whether computational reasoning should be

re-evaluated.



Automatic rebinding SHALL NOT occur.



\---



\# 12. Computational Dependencies



Every Computational Artifact possesses epistemic dependencies.



Examples include:



\- Author Documents

\- Knowledge Objects

\- Presentations

\- Narratives

\- Future computational projections



Dependencies SHALL remain computational objects rather than textual citations.



\---



\# 13. Federation



Epistemic Bindings naturally extend across federated manifolds.



Example:



```

Organization Manifold



↓



Knowledge Object



↓



LIVE Binding



↓



Account Author Document

```



Ownership remains independent.



Epistemic relationships remain intact.



Federation therefore preserves computational understanding without requiring

duplication.



\---



\# 14. Historical Reconstruction



The primary objective of LOCKED Binding is reconstruction of historical

understanding.



Researchers must be capable of determining:



\- what knowledge existed,

\- which Epistemic State informed reasoning,

\- why conclusions were considered reasonable,

\- and how understanding subsequently evolved.



Historical reasoning therefore becomes computationally reproducible.



\---



\# 15. Computational Principles



The following principles are normative.



1\. Every Computational Reference SHALL possess an Epistemic Binding.



2\. Computational References SHALL reference understanding rather than copied

&#x20;  information.



3\. LIVE Bindings SHALL follow evolving Epistemic States.



4\. LOCKED Bindings SHALL preserve the Epistemic State upon which reasoning was

&#x20;  intentionally performed.



5\. Publication SHALL preserve computational reproducibility.



6\. Operators SHALL intentionally determine whether to adopt newer Epistemic

&#x20;  States.



7\. Automatic rebinding of LOCKED references SHALL NOT occur.



8\. Historical reasoning SHALL remain computationally reconstructable.



\---



\# 16. Architectural Summary



Computational Epistemic Binding transforms references from passive citations

into active computational relationships.



Rather than preserving only documents, iSEES preserves the evolution of

understanding itself.



LIVE Bindings support ongoing research.



LOCKED Bindings preserve the historical state of knowledge upon which reasoning

was intentionally performed.



Together these mechanisms establish the canonical reference architecture for

all computational artifacts within the iSEES platform.
